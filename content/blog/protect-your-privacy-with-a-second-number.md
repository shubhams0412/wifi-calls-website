Your phone number is more personal than it looks. It's linked to your messaging apps, your accounts and often your name. Once it's shared, you can't easily take it back. A **second phone number** gives you a buffer: a number you can hand out freely, while your real one stays with the people you trust.

Here's how to use one to protect your privacy in everyday situations, and the tools in Wi-Fi Calling & Texting that help.

## Why your real number is worth protecting

When you share your personal number, you also share a way to reach you directly, at any time. You may end up with:

- Calls and texts from strangers long after a sale or chat is over
- Promotional messages from every site that asked for your number
- Spam calls you can't easily escape without changing your number

Changing your personal number is a hassle, because every friend, bank and service needs the new one. Keeping a separate number for less-trusted contacts avoids that problem altogether.

## Situations where a second number helps

### Selling online

Marketplace listings, local selling groups and classified ads often ask for a phone number. Use your second number instead:

- Buyers can call or text you as usual.
- When the sale is done, you can simply stop replying, or [block the number](/features/block-numbers) if someone keeps messaging.
- Your personal number never appears in a listing.

### Dating

Moving a conversation off a dating app usually means swapping numbers. A second number lets you keep chatting by text or call **before** you're ready to share your personal one. If things don't work out, the other person can't reach your real number.

### Sign-ups, deliveries and one-off services

Loyalty programs, food delivery, contractors, event tickets: lots of services want a phone number. Giving them your second number keeps follow-up calls and messages off your main line. Your personal number is still there for your bank and your closest contacts.

### Work and side projects

If you freelance or run a small business from your phone, a dedicated number keeps clients and customers separate from friends and family, and keeps your personal number out of public listings.

## Privacy tools in Wi-Fi Calling & Texting

### Block numbers

If someone won't stop calling or texting, block them in **Settings → Call Settings → Blocked numbers**. You can type in a number or pick a contact. Blocked numbers can't call or text you, and you can unblock them at any time from the same list.

You can also block a number directly from an SMS conversation using the menu in the top corner.

### Anonymous calling (Pro)

Sometimes you need to call someone without showing your number at all. With **Anonymous call** turned on in **Call Settings**, your number is hidden from the person you're calling. This is part of the Pro plan; see [anonymous calling](/features/anonymous-calling).

### Voicemail instead of picking up (Pro)

With [voicemail](/features/voicemail) turned on, callers can leave a message when you don't answer. Then you decide whether to call back. You can use the default greeting or record your own, so you don't have to say your name if you'd rather not.

### Free, private chats with people you trust

Calls and chats with other app users are free. If you allow contact access, the app checks which of your contacts already use Wi-Fi Calling & Texting. Your contacts are uploaded only to find matches, then deleted right away, and they're never sold.

## Good habits for keeping your number private

A second number works best combined with a few simple habits:

- **Decide which number goes where.** Personal number for family, close friends, banks and anything security-related; second number for everything else.
- **Use your second number in public places.** Listings, social profiles, flyers and sign-up forms.
- **Don't reply to unknown texts with personal details.** Treat unexpected messages with caution, whichever number they arrive on.
- **Block early.** If a conversation turns unpleasant, block the number rather than waiting it out.
- **Check your app settings occasionally.** Review your blocked numbers and, on Pro, your voicemail greeting and anonymous calling setting.

## What a second number doesn't do

It's worth being clear about the limits:

- A second number protects your **phone number**, not other personal details. Be careful what you share in conversations.
- Your personal number and SIM keep working as normal. A second number doesn't change or hide your carrier number when you call from your phone's regular dialer.
